# How to handle meetings and follow-ups

Nothing here ever gets sent, scheduled, or marked done without an explicit yes from the person it is for. That boundary matters more than the format of the brief itself.

## Before a meeting: the brief

Find the meeting first, from a connected calendar if one exists, or by asking who, when, and what it is about if not. Recall each attendee and their organization, along with past meetings with them, open commitments running in both directions, and decisions already made; the memories themselves get opened and read rather than relied on for their one-line descriptions alone. If an email or chat tool is connected, the recent messages with these people get searched and actually read, never guessed at from a subject line.

The brief itself fits on one screen: who each person is, their role, and the history with them in two lines each; what this particular meeting is for and the outcome wanted from it; open items in both directions with their dates; two to four questions or points worth raising; and anything sensitive that needs care or avoidance. Anything that could not be confirmed gets marked as such rather than stated as fact.

## After a meeting: turning notes into commitments

When notes, a transcript, or a summary come in: every decision and every commitment gets pulled out as who, what, and by when; a commitment with no date attached gets recorded as having no date set rather than being assigned an invented one. That list gets shown before anything else happens, so it can be corrected. Once confirmed, decisions and commitments become memories linked to the relevant person or project, following [[reference_how_to_save_a_memory]], so a later recall brings them back automatically. A follow-up message summarizing what was agreed can be offered, but it only gets sent on an explicit yes, never on the assumption that offering it was enough.

## Chasing open items

When someone asks what is still owed, or ahead of any meeting: open commitments get recalled, with the most overdue ones listed first and how late each one is. For each, the next step, whether that is waiting, a gentle reminder, or an escalation, gets proposed along with a drafted message. Nothing gets sent, no calendar event gets created, and nothing gets marked done without a yes first. Once something is actually delivered, its memory gets updated with the date.

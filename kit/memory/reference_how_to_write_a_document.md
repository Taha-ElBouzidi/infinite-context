# How to write a document someone else will read

A document fails when it is written for the writer instead of the reader. Everything below starts from the reader: what they already know, what they need, and how much time they will actually give it.

## Before writing

Settle these, asking only for whatever is missing:

- Who reads it, what they already know, and how much time they will give it.
- What it must achieve: the decision, action, or understanding it should produce.
- The facts it depends on. Recall what is already known before asking for the rest, and never invent a number, a name, or a date. Mark anything still missing clearly and list it at the end rather than guessing at it.
- Any constraints: length, language, tone, or a template the document must follow.

## Outline before drafting

Write the outline first: the headings, and under each heading the one point it makes. Put the answer or the ask first; background comes after, not before. For anything longer than a page, show the outline and let it be changed before drafting starts. Changing an outline costs a minute. Changing a finished draft costs an hour.

## Drafting

- One idea per paragraph, with the point in its first sentence.
- Plain words, short sentences, and a table for anything with more than three numbers in it.
- Say who does what by when, wherever action is needed.
- Match the reader's context and the author's own voice rather than a generic register.

## Testing it as the reader

Read the draft again as the reader would, or hand it to an independent reviewer with only the reader's description and the document itself, the way described in [[reference_how_to_review_work_before_calling_it_done]]. Ask three things: after a thirty second skim, what would they think it says, and is that the actual point; what would they ask that it does not answer; and what could they misread. Fix what that surfaces, then hand the document over along with the list of open items and any assumption that was made along the way.

## What lasts beyond the document

Decisions, figures, and commitments that the document fixes in place belong in memory once it is done, following [[reference_how_to_save_a_memory]], with the document's location noted as the source.

# How to write a first message to someone new

The reader has no reason yet to answer, so the message must give one within a few seconds. This covers email and chat. Once the person replies, their own writing replaces every guess: match their language, register and length.

## What good looks like

- Short: 50 to 125 words by email, with a subject of nine words or fewer; by chat, two or three short sentences, then wait for the answer.
- Why this person: one line on why the owner writes to them in particular, from something real and public, such as a role or a published program.
- Who the owner is: one line, only the part that matters to this reader.
- One clear ask that is easy to answer: a yes or no, a name, one piece of information or a short call. If it fits, add an easy way out: "If you are not the right person, who is?"

## Steps, in order

1. **Check the history.** Recall the person, their company and people like them: did the owner already write, when, and how did they answer? If the owner already wrote to this reader about this ask, stop: it is a follow-up (see below), not a first message.
2. **Learn who the reader is:** role, company, language, channel. Read what they publish, such as a site or a partner page, and note what was found and where ([[reference_how_to_research_with_sources]]).
3. **Write a three-line reader profile before drafting:** language and formality; how much relationship before business; how direct the ask can be. Evidence from earlier replies beats general ideas about a country or culture, which are only hints.
4. **Draft in this order:** why them, who the owner is, the ask. Say only what serves the owner with this reader.
5. **Check it.** By email, run `node tools/email-check.mjs draft.txt --first`: it fails over 125 words, more than two questions, any list line, a sentence over 30 words, common AI phrases, a long subject and signs of price shopping. For chat, check by eye: two or three short sentences, one ask. Then read it as the reader: how will they probably react? Too much, too pushy, too vague, not for them? Revise.
6. **Show the draft and send only on an explicit yes** ([[feedback_reversible_act_irreversible_ask]]).

## Mistakes to avoid, and what to do instead

| Instead of | Do this |
|---|---|
| A numbered or bulleted list of questions, even when the profile suggests it would suit the reader | Sentences, and the one ask that matters now; the needs stay, the shape changes |
| Asking what their site already says | Read it first, then ask only what is not published |
| Saying the owner compares offers, holds others or knows the public price | Write as a committed partner: volumes, plans, the terms needed |

## Follow-up timing

- Wait at least three days. About a week of silence is a real reason to follow up.
- Follow up only for a reason: an answer still needed, or a promised reply overdue. Not to fill silence, not to repeat a question already answered, not several in a row. Read the whole thread first.
- Name the first message by date, restate the ask in one short sentence, add anything new, and give an easy way out ("If this is no longer relevant, no problem"). Never restart the pitch.

## Why

The word range comes from a 2016 study of 40 million emails, where replies peaked near 75 to 100 words. A list reads like a chore handed to a stranger. Mentioning other offers marks the owner as a price shopper and invites a quote just under the public price. In one test, step 1 found an earlier unanswered email to the same company, so the right draft was a follow-up.
